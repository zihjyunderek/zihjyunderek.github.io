A resident survey answers how people feel about their city, but a map needs a score for every census tract, including tracts where few people answered. The City Satisfaction Score bridges the two, and it arrived as a composite across three cities with no methodology documentation. Rather than treat it as a black box, I derived its structure from the data itself and built a pipeline that re-verifies that structure on every run.

> The delivered data is confidential under NDA. The figure and table on this page use simulated files with the same structure.

## From answers to a tract score

Survey answers are sparse and uneven across tracts. The geo-bootstrap builds each tract's score from a sample of nearby respondents, drawn so that the sample matches the tract's own population on two demographic traits at a time, with census shares as quotas and closer respondents more likely to be drawn. The draw is repeated many times and averaged, separately for car, walk and transit users, and the three combine by the tract's commuting shares. A pairing of traits only counts when its sample matches the census within a set tolerance.

## Reverse-engineering, then locking it down

The delivered files contained the scores but not the recipe. By working backward from the numbers, I recovered the relationship: per travel mode (car, walk, transit), a mode score is the mean of a four-question satisfaction battery over geo-bootstrapped tract means; those mode scores then combine through per-tract travel-mode weights into the composite.

The crucial engineering decision: **the derived relationships are re-verified numerically at every pipeline run**, across all three cities, to a maximum absolute error of 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ (CSV rounding precision). So if an upstream input ever changes in a way that breaks the assumed structure, the pipeline catches it immediately rather than silently producing wrong scores. That's the difference between reverse-engineering something once and being able to *depend* on it.

## The method on simulated files

The delivered files held three things per tract: twelve question means (four per travel mode), three mode weights, and the composite score. Each candidate structure rebuilds the composite from the first two and is judged by its largest error against the third.

A structure that fits once can still break when an upstream input changes. So every run recomputes the largest error in each city and compares it with the error that rounding alone could cause.
