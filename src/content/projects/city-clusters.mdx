Describing a city tract by tract produces too many numbers to read. Clustering condenses them into a few **urban archetypes**: groups of neighborhoods that look alike on many measures at once. Rather than trust one method, the pipeline runs seven algorithms across three families and reports them side by side, then settles on K-Medoids for the archetypes themselves.

> The 137 isochrone metrics and the client results are confidential under NDA. The figures on this page rerun the same steps on twelve public American Community Survey indicators for New York City.

## The approach

In the client work each tract is described by 137 isochrone metrics, what can be reached within a walk, a transit ride or a drive, covering mobility, land use, economic conditions, environmental quality and urban form. Each family gets the preprocessing its assumptions need:

| Family | Algorithms | Features | Preprocessing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Numerical | K-Means, GMM, Ward | 30 curated | Yeo-Johnson power transform |
| Spatial | K-Medoids (FasterPAM) | about 132 | Robust scaling and a fused distance |
| Density | DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, OPTICS | 12 orthogonal | Yeo-Johnson power transform |

For every method and every K the pipeline computes three validity indices and writes everything into one self-contained dashboard per city.

## Deliberate choices

- **K-Medoids for the archetypes.** A medoid is a real tract, not an average, so every archetype has a neighborhood one can point to and describe.
- **A fused distance.** Distance in the features is blended with distance on the ground, so tracts that are both alike and near land together.
- **Robust scaling.** Centering on the median and scaling by the interquartile range keeps outlier tracts, with extreme rent or pollution, from dominating.
- **FasterPAM.** The `kmedoids` implementation of FasterPAM (Schubert and Rousseeuw, 2021) is fast and reproducible at city scale.

## New York City, step by step

The three families are run on twelve census indicators per tract, from population density and income to commuting and housing age. The first question is how many clusters the data supports.

No algorithm finds sharply separated groups: the silhouette never exceeds 0.24. It peaks at two or three clusters for every algorithm and declines slowly after that, so the indices alone do not settle K. At five clusters it stays between 0.14 and 0.20, with K-Medoids highest. For K-Medoids itself, five is the best of the solutions with four or more clusters, and the archetypes below use it.

Density-based methods take no K. They find dense regions and leave the rest as noise.

On this data they find one or two clusters. DBSCAN keeps 97% of tracts in two clusters, OPTICS puts every tract in one, and HDBSCAN finds two dense cores and leaves 90% of tracts as noise. Neighborhood indicators change gradually from tract to tract, so the archetypes below are cuts through a continuum, not islands.

The four solutions agree only in part: the adjusted Rand index runs from 0.36 to 0.52. Ward and GMM are closest to each other. K-Medoids, the only one that also weighs distance on the ground, agrees with the others at 0.36 to 0.41. That is why every algorithm is reported side by side.

## The K-Medoids archetypes

The geography weight decides how far closeness on the ground counts.

Each archetype has a medoid, the tract with the smallest total distance to the other members. It is a real place with real numbers, not an average.

Archetype 1 is dense and poor: income 1.2 standard deviations below the city mean, poverty 1.2 above, mostly renters who commute by transit. Archetype 2 is dense and affluent, with the highest income, rent and share of college graduates. Archetype 3, the largest group, sits near the city mean with more foreign-born residents and more crowded homes. Archetypes 4 and 5 are the low-density edges: fewer renters, fewer transit commuters, more households with a car. The medoids make this concrete. Tract 83 in the Bronx, with a median income of $53,560 and a median rent of $1,356, stands for Archetype 1; Tract 563.01 in Brooklyn, at $146,458 and $2,780, for Archetype 2.
