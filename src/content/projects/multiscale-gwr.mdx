Conventional regression assumes one relationship holds everywhere: that an extra point of income changes rents by the same amount in Manhattan as in Staten Island. Cities do not work that way. **Multiscale Geographically Weighted Regression (MGWR)** fits a coefficient for every place and lets each predictor choose the distance over which its relationship holds. The result is a coefficient surface per variable, not a single number.

> I run this workflow across US cities in the City Design Index, whose data is confidential under NDA. The example below runs the same steps on public American Community Survey data for New York City.

## The workflow

- **Variable selection.** Forward selection on GWR's AICc, with the bandwidth searched again at every step.
- **Calibration.** OLS, GWR and MGWR on the same predictors, adaptive bisquare kernels, bandwidths chosen by AICc.
- **Diagnostics.** Model comparison (adjusted R², AICc, ENP), global and local collinearity, significance corrected for multiple tests, and Moran's I on the residuals.
- **Maps.** Coefficient surfaces masked by significance, and standardized residuals for every model.

## New York City, step by step

The example asks what goes with a tract's median gross rent. Eight census indicators are candidates; every variable is standardized.

Household income enters first and already brings the adjusted R² to 0.725. Median age, the foreign-born share, the share of homes built before 1940 and the share of adults with a college degree follow, lowering AICc from 3,625 to 3,483, and the selected bandwidth grows from 46 to 199 tracts as predictors are added. Selection stopped at the cap of five predictors.

The five predictors are then fitted three ways: one coefficient for the whole city (OLS), local coefficients with one shared bandwidth (GWR), and local coefficients with a bandwidth for each predictor (MGWR).

<Figure

  source="Same tracts and predictors as Figure 1."

One city-wide coefficient per predictor explains 65% of the variation in log rent and leaves residuals that cluster on the map (Moran's I 0.111). Letting coefficients vary raises the adjusted R² to 0.729 with one shared bandwidth and to 0.747 with a bandwidth for each predictor, and the clustering disappears. MGWR has the lowest AICc, 3,389 against 3,483 for GWR. Its residual Moran's I is slightly negative (−0.024, p = 0.012): small, but worth watching when a bandwidth is as narrow as 44 tracts.

GWR settles on 199 tracts for every term. MGWR spreads the terms from 44 to 1,015 tracts. Income works at the smallest scale: its link with rent changes within a few dozen tracts. Education works at the largest: the 1,015 nearest tracts, almost half the city, inform each local estimate.

The college-degree coefficient is positive and significant in nearly every tract, between 0.08 and 0.25: a broad, steady premium. The income coefficient is significant in 58% of tracts and ranges from 0.28 to 1.40 where it is. Median age is the one negative term, from −0.58 to −0.13, significant in 24% of tracts. The foreign-born share and pre-1940 homes matter in pockets only, in 14% and 11% of tracts. These are associations in one cross-section, not causal effects.

## Checks on the local estimates

<Figure
  data="diagnostics.csv"
  type="table"
  title="Collinearity, scale and significance by term"
  caption="A VIF above 10 is the usual warning sign of collinearity; the local VIF repeats the check inside every GWR neighborhood. The last column counts MGWR estimates significant at 5% after the correction for multiple tests."
  source="Same model as Table 1. Bandwidths in nearest tracts."
/>

City-wide, no predictor is collinear: the largest VIF is 2.37. Inside GWR neighborhoods the margin is thinner. Household income and college degree reach local VIFs of 10.48 and 10.57 in their worst neighborhoods, just past the usual threshold, so their local estimates should be read together.

Under OLS, neighboring tracts tend to err in the same direction. Under GWR and MGWR the colors mix, which is what the drop in Moran's I in Table 1 measures. The change is subtle to the eye, which is why the statistic is reported beside every residual map.
